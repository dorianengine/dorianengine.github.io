OFEU arcade
Hi, today I will be presenting OFEU arcade, a game that was pitched a year ago in class and started during the month of July 2026.
The pitch
The pitch is simple: save the school on fire. It’s an arcade game where you get points from saving the students and extinguishing the fire. On the other hand, you lose points over time to add more challenge.
At first this came up as an exercise to understand Unreal Engine, but throughout my study I couldn’t get the thought out of my head: “I could totally do OFEU as I first conceptualized it on Pico 8 with the score system and the student and fire we have to save!” So with the help of online training to learn all the basics of Unreal Engine, some Reddit posts, and YouTube tutorials, I made several systems, explained below.
The game mechanics
In this game, you can save students and extinguish fire. As hard as it may seem, the logic behind those mechanics is simple:
  • When you enter the collision box,
  • the input E will be unlocked,
  • then press E to “collect” the student,
  • which triggers the function of giving points,
  • and makes the whole blueprint disappear from the level so that it cannot be triggered again.
The level design mapping
I first mapped it out on Miro to then transfer it into Unreal. The goal was to create chaos and a sense of being stuck. When you see a student, he is trapped behind fires, unable to escape, which makes it logical that he is there and cannot move. The map and fire placement are inspired by the original Pac-Man game, and by a more 3D based game: Dark Deception.
The score and door system

The idea was not to make a hard game. It was to test the blueprint and then fine-tune the points given so that the game is challenging but not impossible.

The collectible that was giving the most points was the student, which is accessible but at a lower rate than the fire.

At first, the fire was supposed to lower your health points, but afterward, a better usage of the fire was that if you enter its core hitbox, you would bounce. Later on, a point penalty was added for every time you bumped into the fire.

To make the map more dynamic, while still focusing on the score, I made doors that either open when the player is close by, or doors that need to be activated by buttons in the room. This slows down the player, forcing the player to plan his moves in order to get the best amount of points.

Upcoming updates
After receiving feedback, I want to improve the score system and use it to create new gameplay features for in game or post game content.

First update (v0.2, out now):

  • The time will progressively take away more points as the game goes on.

Next update:

  • Adding an event, based on point amount.
  • Making new and challenging maps, based on speed and optimization.
  • Creating a leaderboard system (on a local level, not online).
